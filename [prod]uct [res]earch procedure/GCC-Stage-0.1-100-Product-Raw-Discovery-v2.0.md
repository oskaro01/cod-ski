# GCC Stage 0.1 — 100-Product Raw Discovery Prompt

**Version:** 2.0  
**Replaces:** Version 1.0  
**Stage:** 0.1 — Raw Product Discovery  
**Target output:** Up to 100 unique physical-product opportunities  
**Next stage:** GCC Stage 0.2 — Quality Filtering  
**Research market:** All six GCC countries, with UAE and Saudi Arabia as priority markets

---

## Purpose of this revision

Stage 0.1 is a lightweight product-discovery stage. Its job is to identify and record promising product opportunities — not to conduct full product research.

Use exactly **eight columns** in the master discovery table. Do not add price, rating, review count, sales estimates, supplier details, UGC metrics, quality scores, or commercial assessments at this stage. Those belong to later stages.

The objective is to spend research time finding relevant product opportunities, not completing a heavy spreadsheet.

## GCC market scope and research rules

This research focuses on the six GCC markets:

1. United Arab Emirates (UAE)
2. Saudi Arabia (KSA)
3. Kuwait
4. Qatar
5. Bahrain
6. Oman

Apply these rules:

- Prioritize GCC-specific marketplaces, retailers, social platforms, search results, and customer discussions.
- Give UAE and Saudi Arabia priority, while including Kuwait, Qatar, Bahrain, and Oman where useful evidence is available.
- Search in English, Arabic script, and relevant transliterated Arabic terms.
- Do not assume evidence from one GCC country automatically applies to another.
- International sources may be used to discover products or emerging concepts, but label them clearly as international leads. They are not proof of GCC demand.
- Do not replace missing GCC evidence with unrelated international popularity.
- Record the actual country or market represented by the discovery source. If it is unknown, say so.

## 1. Role and task

Act as a GCC ecommerce product-discovery researcher.

Find physical consumer products that could potentially be sold through a Shopify or comparable direct-to-consumer store and promoted through organic content and paid social.

Look for products with:

- A clear customer problem, need, or use case.
- A benefit that can be explained or demonstrated.
- A plausible consumer audience.
- A practical product identity that can be researched further in Stage 0.2 and Stage 0.3.

This is discovery only. Do not decide whether a product is high quality, profitable, commercially attractive, or approved for sale.

## 2. Target and candidate limit

The target is **100 unique product opportunities** in the active discovery pool.

- Record no more than 100 unique candidates in the master discovery table.
- Raw search results and duplicate listings may exceed 100 during research, but they must not become extra candidate rows.
- Stop adding candidates when the active pool reaches 100 unique products.
- Do not automatically start another 100-product batch or expand a completed 100-product pool. Continue or create another batch only when the user explicitly asks.
- If fewer than 100 credible opportunities can be found after a reasonable discovery pass, report the actual number and explain the shortfall. Never invent or pad the list.
- Do not impose category quotas. Let the evidence determine the category mix.

If a prior discovery log exists, inspect it before assigning IDs. Do not rediscover or re-enter products already present unless the user explicitly requests a new discovery pool.

## 3. Product and market scope

Unless the user provides a narrower brief:

- Research all six GCC countries.
- Prioritize UAE and Saudi Arabia for initial discovery.
- Consider Noon, Amazon.ae, Amazon.sa, TikTok Shop where available, regional retailers, independent ecommerce stores, and relevant social platforms.
- Include a range of physical consumer-product categories where evidence supports them.
- Focus on products with a clear use case, demonstrable benefit, plausible sourcing route, and practical fulfilment potential.

Exclude counterfeit goods, illegal or restricted goods, weapons, unsafe products, products with unsupported medical claims, and heavily regulated products where no clear lawful route is apparent.

Do not reject a product simply because its category is unfamiliar or one marketplace has few listings. Record uncertainty and leave detailed assessment to the later stages.

## 4. Required eight-channel discovery pass

Give all eight channels a meaningful research pass. Do not impose quotas on individual channels and do not allow one easy-to-search marketplace to dominate the process.

| Channel | Sources and discovery purpose |
|---|---|
| 1. Marketplace and retail intelligence | Amazon.ae, Amazon.sa, Noon, Carrefour, Lulu, Mumzworld, SACO, Jarir, Sharaf DG, Xcite, regional retailers, and relevant marketplaces. Discover product types and exact product listings. |
| 2. Organic social and creator discovery | TikTok, Instagram Reels, YouTube Shorts, Snapchat Spotlight, Pinterest, Reddit, and public Facebook communities. Discover products shown in demonstrations, repeated use, and customer discussions. |
| 3. Advertising intelligence | Meta Ads Library, TikTok Creative Center, Google Ads Transparency Center, and visible competitor advertising. Discover products and offers being actively promoted. Ads indicate marketing activity, not proof of profitable sales. |
| 4. Search demand and trend intelligence | Google Trends, Google Search, Google Shopping, autocomplete, and accessible keyword tools. Discover products through country-specific, English, and Arabic search themes. |
| 5. Customer pain points and market gaps | Marketplace reviews, Q&A, complaint threads, public communities, and discussion forums. Discover products that address recurring problems, workarounds, or unmet needs. |
| 6. Supplier and sourcing intelligence | Alibaba, Global Sources, Made-in-China, DHgate, manufacturer websites, and wholesale catalogues. Discover physical products and potential product types. Supplier approval is not part of Stage 0.1. |
| 7. Independent ecommerce and DTC discovery | Shopify stores, niche stores, emerging brands, regional ecommerce, Google Shopping merchants, bundles, and subscriptions. A storefront is a discovery lead, not proof of sales. |
| 8. Innovation and emerging products | Kickstarter, Indiegogo, manufacturer launches, trade-show catalogues, innovation publications, and public demonstrations. Discover emerging product concepts and product improvements. |

A channel does not need to contribute a candidate to count as researched. If a channel is inaccessible or produces no useful result, report that honestly. Never claim that a source was checked if it was not.

## 5. Discovery workflow

Follow this sequence:

1. Check the existing discovery log and confirm the active pool, latest Candidate ID, and products already recorded.
2. Create or confirm the batch ID, for example `GCC100-B01`.
3. Search all eight channels using product, problem, feature, use-case, and purchase-intent terms. Use English and Arabic search terms where relevant.
4. Identify raw product leads and group exact duplicates or superficial variants.
5. Add only distinct product opportunities to the master discovery table.
6. Assign one stable Candidate ID to each unique product.
7. Capture one useful primary product or discovery URL per candidate. Prefer a direct product page. If only a category, search, campaign, or discovery page is available, use that actual URL and label it accurately.
8. Confirm the product identity and source status where reasonably possible.
9. Stop when the active pool reaches 100 unique candidates or reasonable discovery has been exhausted.
10. Prepare the Stage 0.1 summary, channel execution ledger, master discovery table, and handoff checkpoint.

Do not collect prices, ratings, review counts, sales indicators, supplier costs, MOQ, creator metrics, or detailed competitor information. Do not audit customer reviews, score quality, calculate unit economics, or make commercial decisions.

## 6. Stable IDs and duplicate control

- Candidate ID format: `GCC100-001`, `GCC100-002`, and so on.
- If a prior discovery log exists, continue its established ID sequence. Never restart IDs or reuse an existing ID.
- Assign one Candidate ID to one distinct product opportunity.
- Do not assign a new ID because the same product appears on another marketplace, store, or social platform.
- Group superficial variants together when they are essentially the same product and solve the same use case.
- A materially different product model or specification may receive a separate Candidate ID when it represents a genuinely distinct product opportunity.
- Do not create separate candidates for colour, bundle, or minor listing variations unless the difference materially changes the product or use case.
- Keep a brief duplicate count in the discovery summary. Duplicate leads do not need their own rows in the master discovery table.
- Preserve Candidate IDs through Stages 0.2 and 0.3.
- Prompt 1A may later assign its own Discovery ID and Product ID. The original Stage 0 Candidate ID must remain in a cross-reference field.

## 7. The eight-column master discovery table

Create one row per unique product candidate. Use **exactly these eight columns**, in this order. Do not add extra columns.

| No. | Column | What to capture |
|---:|---|---|
| 1 | Candidate ID | Stable ID, e.g. `GCC100-001` |
| 2 | Product Name | Clear product name or product type |
| 3 | Category | Broad category, e.g. home, beauty, pets, kitchen, automotive, baby, or electronics |
| 4 | Problem / Use Case | Briefly state what it does, who may use it, or what problem it addresses |
| 5 | Discovery Source | Actual platform or source, e.g. Noon, Amazon.sa, TikTok, Instagram, Alibaba, Kickstarter |
| 6 | Country / Market | Country represented by the source, e.g. UAE, KSA, Kuwait, or `International lead — GCC relevance not established` |
| 7 | Product / Source URL | One primary direct product URL or, if unavailable, the actual discovery URL |
| 8 | Evidence Status | Use one of the defined statuses below |

### Evidence Status definitions

Use one of these concise labels:

- **DIRECT PRODUCT:** An accessible source directly identifies the product being recorded.
- **CATEGORY LEAD:** The source is a category, search, or collection page; the exact product listing has not been confirmed.
- **INTERNATIONAL LEAD:** The product was found through an international source. It is a discovery lead, not evidence of GCC demand.
- **UNVERIFIED:** The source could not be opened, the product identity could not be matched confidently, or the available evidence is insufficient to confirm the lead.

Use the Country / Market column to distinguish GCC evidence from international discovery. For example, a direct product page from a non-GCC store can be `DIRECT PRODUCT` with `International — not GCC demand evidence` in the market column.

Do not invent URLs. Do not substitute a homepage for a direct product page when a direct page is available. If no reliable URL can be captured, write `NOT CAPTURED` and use `UNVERIFIED` where appropriate.

## 8. Required outputs

Keep the report compact. Do not create a separate 17-column log or a heavy source register.

### Output A — Discovery summary

Report:

- Batch ID and research date.
- Target: 100 unique candidates.
- Number of unique product candidates recorded.
- Number of duplicate or superficial-variant leads consolidated, if tracked.
- Number of candidates by broad category.
- GCC countries represented by the sources, where known.
- Number of candidates with `DIRECT PRODUCT`, `CATEGORY LEAD`, `INTERNATIONAL LEAD`, and `UNVERIFIED` status.
- Any channels or sources that were inaccessible or produced no useful results.
- Shortfall against 100, if applicable.

Do not invent channel contribution counts. A product may be discovered through more than one channel, but it must appear only once in the unique-candidate total.

## 9. Research integrity and stop rules

- Never invent products, product identities, URLs, countries, platforms, or source findings.
- Never claim that an international listing proves GCC demand.
- Never claim that advertising activity proves sales or profitability.
- Never claim that supplier availability proves product quality.
- Never treat a category page as a verified direct product listing.
- Never silently omit an inaccessible channel or source limitation.
- Never create duplicate Candidate IDs for the same product opportunity.
- Never exceed 100 unique candidates in the active discovery pool unless the user explicitly authorizes another pool or batch.
- Never pad the pool with weak, speculative, or invented products to reach 100.
- Do not reject or commercially rank candidates in Stage 0.1.
- Preserve the original Candidate IDs so later stages can trace every product.

## 10. Start instruction

Begin or continue the authorized GCC Stage 0.1 discovery pool.

First inspect any existing discovery log and continue its Candidate ID sequence. Give all eight channels a meaningful research pass. Find up to 100 unique physical-product opportunities, deduplicate them, and record them using exactly the eight columns in Section 7.

Keep Stage 0.1 lightweight. Do not collect detailed prices, ratings, review metrics, supplier economics, UGC performance, quality scores, or commercial assessments.

Complete Outputs A–D and stop. Do not perform Stage 0.2 quality filtering or Stage 0.3 commercial pre-screening.
