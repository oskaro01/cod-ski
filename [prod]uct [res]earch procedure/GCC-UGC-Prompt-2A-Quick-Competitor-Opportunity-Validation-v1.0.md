# Prompt 2A: Quick Competitor Product Opportunity Validation

**Version:** 1.0  
**Workflow position:** Optional branch after Prompt 2 — Individual
Product Deep Research  
**Purpose:** Decide whether a competitor product discovered during
Prompt 2 deserves a full, separate Prompt 2 research cycle  
**Research market:** All six GCC countries, prioritizing UAE and Saudi
Arabia

------------------------------------------------------------------------

## 1. Purpose and operating principle

Use this prompt only when the user is reviewing a completed or
in-progress Prompt 2 research and personally notices a competitor or
comparable product that looks unusually interesting.

This is a **quick opportunity-validation gate**, not another full
product-research prompt.

Its single question is:

> Is this alternative product promising enough to justify spending a
> full Prompt 2 research cycle on it?

Do not perform the full Prompt 2 workflow here. Do not produce a full
supplier audit, detailed demand model, full unit economics, sample plan,
or deep research dossier. The existing Prompt 2 remains the full
research process.

The original product and its Prompt 2 research must remain untouched.
The alternative is assessed as a separate opportunity, not an automatic
replacement.

## 2. Required input

Check the supplied product/opportunity registry for duplicates before research. Another seller URL for the same product is supporting evidence, not a new opportunity. Treat a variant as separate only when its identity or commercial proposition materially differs. If the original Product ID is unknown, assess the alternative on its own merits and label the comparison unavailable.

The primary required input is:

- **Competitor’s Direct Product URL:** a product-specific page for the
  alternative being considered.

Also provide where available:

- Competitor/product name and brand.
- Original Product ID and product name that led to the discovery.
- What caught the user’s attention or why the alternative looks
  interesting.
- Target GCC country or countries. If not specified, use all six GCC
  countries, prioritizing UAE and KSA.

If the original Product ID is unknown, record `NOT PROVIDED`; do not
invent one. If the user has not supplied a reason for interest,
independently explain what appears potentially interesting from the
available evidence.

Do not begin with a category page, search-result page, generic homepage,
social post, or advertisement as the competitor’s product URL. Those
sources may help locate the actual product page, but cannot replace it.

## 3. GCC market scope

Focus on:

1.  United Arab Emirates (UAE)
2.  Saudi Arabia (KSA)
3.  Kuwait
4.  Qatar
5.  Bahrain
6.  Oman

Prioritize early signals from UAE and Saudi Arabia. Check other GCC
markets when relevant evidence is readily available.

This is an early opportunity check, not full GCC demand validation:

- Record the country, platform, currency, direct source URL and date
  checked.
- Separate GCC evidence from international evidence.
- Do not treat international views, ratings, sales claims, or popularity
  as proof of GCC demand.
- Do not assume that evidence from one GCC country applies to all six.
- If no GCC signal is found quickly, record `NOT FOUND`; do not
  manufacture one or expand this into a full market study.

## 4. Product identity and direct-link gate

Before assessing opportunity, verify that the alternative is a real,
identifiable product.

1.  Open the supplied Direct Product URL.
2.  Confirm that the page loads and identifies the actual product.
3.  Capture the product name, brand, model/variant, seller or publisher,
    country/market and visible price where available.
4.  Accept an individual retailer/marketplace listing, official
    manufacturer product page, DTC product page, or product-specific
    crowdfunding page that clearly identifies the actual product.
5.  Do not accept a category page, search-result page, collection page,
    generic homepage, generic campaign page, social post, or
    advertisement as the Direct Product URL.
6.  If the supplied URL is broken, inaccessible or mismatched, search
    for another accessible direct page for the **same product**.
7.  If no direct product page can establish the product identity after a
    reasonable check, do not treat it as a valid opportunity. Use
    `HOLD — NEED MORE EVIDENCE` with reason `PRODUCT PAGE NOT VERIFIED` if a specific access issue may be
    resolved; use `REJECT` if the product itself cannot be identified or
    substantiated.

Use one Product Match Type:

- **EXACT PRODUCT:** the page identifies the exact product/model being
  assessed.
- **COMPARABLE PRODUCT:** the page identifies a different product that
  solves substantially the same customer problem as the original product
  or reference opportunity. The comparable itself is the alternative
  being assessed.
- **VARIANT / MODEL:** the page identifies a specific model or variant
  that materially changes the product opportunity.

A comparable product must have its own direct product page. Never
describe it as the exact original product. Never substitute it into the
original product’s research record.

## 5. Quick opportunity checks

Keep each check proportionate. Use enough current evidence to make a
defensible quick decision, then stop. Do not continue into Prompt
2-level depth.

### A. Product usefulness and customer problem

Identify:

- The main customer problem, desire or inconvenience.
- The intended buyer or use case.
- The product’s main benefit in one clear sentence.
- Whether the benefit is understandable without a long explanation.
- Whether the product appears genuinely useful, attractive or
  meaningfully different from the original product.

Distinguish a real product benefit from marketing language that is not
substantiated by the page or evidence.

### B. Why this alternative caught attention

Compare the alternative with the original researched product, using only
the available context.

Explain:

- What appears more interesting, useful, demonstrable or beneficial
  about the alternative.
- Whether it solves the same problem, a closely related problem, or a
  different problem.
- What benefit or feature could make it worth investigating separately.
- Whether the apparent advantage is supported by evidence or is only an
  initial impression.

Do not assume the alternative is better merely because it is newer, more
expensive, more visually attractive, or from a better-known brand.

### C. Early GCC opportunity signals

Look for a small number of useful early signals, prioritizing UAE and
KSA:

- A direct GCC product listing or retailer offer.
- Visible GCC price, availability, rating or review signal where
  disclosed.
- A relevant local retailer, seller or brand presence.
- A country-relevant search or social signal.
- A GCC customer use case that is supported by evidence.

Record the source, country, date, and what it actually proves. Keep
exact-product and comparable-product evidence separate.

This is not a full demand study. Do not calculate a Demand Index,
conduct a full Google Trends protocol, estimate sales from review
counts, or claim GCC-wide demand from one country.

### D. UGC and creative evidence

UGC is an explicit part of this quick validation. Search for evidence in
this order:

1.  Exact-product or exact-model creator/customer demonstrations.
2.  Comparable-product demonstrations that show the same customer
    problem or benefit.
3.  Category-level content or general trend evidence, clearly labelled
    as indirect.

Check TikTok, Instagram Reels and YouTube Shorts. Check other relevant
creator platforms where accessible.

For each useful item, record:

- Direct video/post URL.
- Platform and creator, if identifiable.
- Publication date or `DATE NOT VISIBLE`.
- Country/audience relevance, only when verifiable.
- Product identity and evidence type: `EXACT PRODUCT`,
  `COMPARABLE PRODUCT`, `CATEGORY`, or `GENERAL TREND`.
- Visible views, likes, comments and shares; use `NOT CAPTURED` when
  unavailable.
- The hook, problem shown, demonstration and benefit communicated.

Assess creative potential:

- Can the product’s benefit be shown quickly and clearly?
- Is there a visual demonstration, transformation, comparison or
  before/after?
- Does the content show the actual product working?
- Could the demonstration be adapted credibly for GCC audiences?
- Are comments or reactions useful evidence of questions, objections or
  interest?

UGC rules:

- Do not invent video links, views, engagement, creators, dates or
  audience geography.
- Do not treat one viral video as proof of demand or profitability.
- Do not treat category-level content as exact-product traction.
- Do not use negative experiences, injuries, fires, failures or safety
  complaints as positive UGC evidence; record them as risks.
- Do not impose an arbitrary viral-view threshold.
- If no exact or comparable UGC is found, record the gap and assess
  whether the product still has strong practical demonstration
  potential. Missing UGC alone does not automatically mean `REJECT`; it
  may support `HOLD` if the opportunity remains uncertain.

### E. Quick commercial and sourcing plausibility

Use only visible, quickly obtainable information:

- Current visible product price and currency.
- Whether the price positioning appears plausible for a GCC consumer
  offer.
- Whether at least one identifiable manufacturer, supplier or legitimate
  sourcing route appears to exist.
- Whether a quick source appears to be an exact match or comparable
  product.
- Any obvious mismatch between the visible product offer and available
  sourcing information.

Do not perform a full supplier search. Do not require three suppliers.
Do not claim wholesale availability, authorization, MOQ, landed cost or
profitability unless directly evidenced.

Do not calculate full contribution, CAC, ROAS, break-even economics,
shipping, customs or VAT. Record important unknowns for Prompt 2.

For branded competitor products, distinguish: - Whether the branded
product itself appears to have a legitimate authorized sourcing route;
and - Whether a legitimate comparable/OEM/private-label product may be
sourceable.

Do not imply that a branded product can be resold without authorization.

### F. Obvious risks and blockers

Check for immediately visible concerns:

- Safety, electrical, battery, chemical, food-contact, skin-contact or
  child-related risk, as relevant.
- Restricted, counterfeit or potentially unlawful product concerns.
- Unsupported medical or performance claims.
- Fragility, size, weight or obvious GCC fulfilment difficulty.
- Unclear product identity, missing specifications or inconsistent
  product claims.
- A product benefit that cannot be demonstrated or explained credibly.

Do not make unsupported legal or compliance conclusions. Mark unresolved
documentation as `PENDING VERIFICATION`.

## 6. Decision rules

Return exactly one of these decisions.

### PROMOTE TO PROMPT 2

Use when the alternative is a real, identifiable product and the quick
evidence shows a sufficiently interesting opportunity to justify full
individual research.

The evidence should explain: - The customer problem and meaningful
benefit. - Why the alternative is worth investigating relative to the
original product. - Relevant early GCC signals, if found. - UGC evidence
or a credible demonstration opportunity. - No obvious blocker that makes
full research pointless.

`PROMOTE TO PROMPT 2` means **worth researching further**. It is not
proof of demand, profitability, supplier approval, sample approval or
permission to purchase.

### HOLD — NEED MORE EVIDENCE

Use when the product looks potentially interesting but one or more
decision-changing facts remain unclear.

State: - What looks promising. - What is missing or conflicting. - The
smallest specific check that could resolve the uncertainty. - Whether
the missing item concerns product identity, GCC relevance, UGC,
sourcing, economics or risk.

Missing evidence alone is not a reason to reject a potentially credible
opportunity.

### REJECT

Use when evidence shows that the product is not a worthwhile opportunity
to research further, for example:

- The actual product cannot be identified or no qualifying direct
  product page can be established.
- The customer problem or benefit is weak, unclear or unsupported.
- The apparent advantage over the original product is not credible.
- There is no plausible demonstration or use case.
- A clear safety, legal, sourcing or fulfilment blocker makes the
  opportunity unsuitable.
- The available evidence gives no credible reason to spend a full Prompt
  2 cycle on it.

Give the evidence-based reason. Do not use `REJECT` merely because a
metric or source is unavailable; use `HOLD` when a specific recovery
check could change the decision.

## 7. Required output — compact opportunity validation

Process one alternative per execution. Target at most 700 words including table explanations, excluding URLs. Record at most two useful UGC references; do not keep searching for a quota. Use relevant UAE/KSA checks first and readily available secondary-market evidence; do not require a six-country study. Stop once a defensible quick verdict is possible. If research is interrupted, give a partial record with checked URLs, findings and the exact remaining action; do not call incomplete work a completed promotion.

Do not produce a full Prompt 2 dossier. Keep the result concise and
decision-focused.

### Output A — Quick verdict

Start with:

``` text

Alternative product:

```

### Output D — Why full Prompt 2 is or is not justified

Give a short evidence-based explanation. State the most important
questions that full Prompt 2 would need to answer if the verdict is
PROMOTE or HOLD.

### Output E — Next action

- **PROMOTE TO PROMPT 2:** The user may approve the alternative for a
  separate full Prompt 2 research cycle. Assign it a new Product ID from
  the project’s authorized Product ID sequence after approval. Preserve
  the original Product ID and all original research. Do not transfer the
  original product’s scores, reviews, supplier costs, UGC metrics or
  conclusions to the alternative.
  Check the cumulative Product ID registry before assigning the next unused ID. If the registry is missing, request it rather than guessing. Pass an entry record containing PROMPT 2A - USER-APPROVED, verdict/date, approval reference, Original Product URL, current Direct Product URL, link status/date, parent product reference, evidence and unresolved questions. A prior Prompt 1B YES or GCC Candidate ID is not required for this route.
- **HOLD — NEED MORE EVIDENCE:** Perform only the specific recovery
  check stated in the verdict, then reassess.
- **REJECT:** Do not spend a full Prompt 2 cycle on it. Keep the concise
  decision record in the competitor-opportunity tracker if that tracker
  is being used.

Do not assign a new GCC100 Candidate ID to a product discovered after
Prompt 2 unless the user explicitly routes it back through Stage 0.1. A
new alternative promoted to individual research receives its own new
Product ID, not the original product’s ID.

## 8. Research integrity and boundaries

- Use current sources and record the date checked.
- Cite direct URLs for all material findings.
- Never invent products, URLs, prices, views, engagement, supplier
  facts, dates, country relevance or specifications.
- Separate exact-product, comparable-product, category and general-trend
  evidence.
- Keep GCC evidence separate from international evidence.
- Treat UGC as evidence of demonstration/creative traction, not
  automatic proof of sales.
- Do not conduct full Prompt 2 research inside this prompt.
- Do not create a full unit-economics model or supplier audit.
- Do not replace, overwrite or downgrade the original product’s Prompt 2
  record.
- Do not automatically run Prompt 2 after a PROMOTE verdict; the user
  decides whether to proceed.
- When the user approves full research, use the existing
  `[light]individual-prod-two-col-markdown-table-after-research-FINAL.md` Prompt
  2 as the deep-research methodology, with its user-approved Prompt 2A entry route, a new Product ID
  and independent evidence.
- Stop after Outputs A–E.
