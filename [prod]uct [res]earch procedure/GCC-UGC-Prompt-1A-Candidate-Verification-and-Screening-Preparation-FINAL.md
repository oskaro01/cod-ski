# Prompt 1A: GCC Product Candidate Verification and Full-Screening Preparation

**Version:** 1.1
**Revises:** Existing FINAL version

## Purpose

Use this prompt only after GCC Stage 0.3 Commercial Pre-Screening has
selected candidates for Prompt 1A.

The product input is Stage 0.3 Output E, with its referenced evidence and checkpoint. Receive only SELECT FOR PROMPT 1A candidates, plus reserves the user explicitly promotes for screening. If evidence fields are missing, research or label the gap; do not demand an unrelated full-pool report when the current-group handoff is sufficient.

Prompt 1A is responsible for verifying and preparing already-selected
Stage 0.3 candidates for Prompt 1B full screening. It is NOT responsible
for discovering another set of products, creating a new shortlist,
selecting replacement candidates, or changing a candidate into a
different product.

Process the Stage 0.3 selected candidates in their existing order, in
groups of up to five candidates per handoff.

For each candidate: - Preserve its original GCC Candidate ID and optional legacy Discovery ID, if supplied. - Preserve its original source references. - Preserve
its Stage 0.2 track, decision, score type and evidence coverage. -
Preserve its Stage 0.3 commercial score and selection decision. - Assign
one stable Product ID (P-001, P-002, etc.) if not already assigned. -
Verify the existing product, competitor, supplier and UGC links. -
Collect missing minimum handoff information where possible. - Carry
unresolved questions into Prompt 1B.

Do not autonomously discover new products, replace selected candidates,
or reject candidates based on a new preparation score.

**Authority rule:** This current Prompt 1A version controls its own
role. If a legacy Stage 0.3 handoff or older instruction says Prompt 1A
may discover, select, or replace candidates, ignore that conflicting
legacy wording. Prompt 1A verifies and prepares the existing selected
candidates only. Prompt 1B remains responsible for the final YES / MAYBE
/ NO / UNPROCESSED screening decision.

If a Stage 0.3 candidate has missing evidence, record the gap instead of
silently replacing it. The direct-product-page eligibility gate below
may block a candidate from the Prompt 1B handoff, but it does not
authorize substitution.

This research is specifically focused on the six GCC markets:

1.  United Arab Emirates (UAE)
2.  Saudi Arabia (KSA)
3.  Kuwait
4.  Qatar
5.  Bahrain
6.  Oman

All product research, market validation, demand assessment, competitor
analysis, pricing research, customer feedback analysis, and commercial
evaluation must be conducted with these GCC markets as the target
market.

- Prioritize GCC-specific marketplaces, retailers, suppliers, customers,
  search trends, and social-commerce evidence.

- Record the country, platform, currency, source URL, and research date
  for relevant findings.

- Do not treat non-GCC marketplace listings, ratings, reviews, sales
  figures, or popularity as proof of GCC demand or GCC customer
  satisfaction.

- International sources may be used for supporting technical
  specifications, manufacturer information, product documentation, and
  global supplier discovery, but clearly distinguish them from GCC
  market evidence.

- Suppliers may be located outside the GCC, provided their ability to
  supply the target GCC market is properly evaluated.

- Assess GCC-specific shipping, landed costs, delivery feasibility,
  returns, warranty, product compliance, and applicable country
  requirements.

- Never assume that evidence from one GCC country automatically applies
  to all six countries.

- If GCC-specific evidence is unavailable, explicitly record the gap
  rather than substituting unrelated international evidence.

## Execution control

The Stage 0.3 current-group candidate handoff is mandatory input. If there are no supplied candidate identities and source records, request them; do not invent a pool. Missing individual fields are gaps to recover or disclose, not a reason to restart discovery.

- Execute only targeted verification of existing Stage 0.3-selected
  candidates and preparation of the current-group Prompt 1B handoff. Do
  not perform new product discovery or candidate selection.
- Keep the final handoff compact and do not repeat the same source or
  finding in multiple sections.
- Preserve every completed GCC Candidate ID, optional legacy Discovery ID, and
  Product ID. Never restart a completed batch or assign a duplicate ID.
- If the response is approaching its limit, stop after the last
  completed research unit and emit a compact checkpoint listing
  completed IDs, remaining work, and the next action. Do not silently
  skip unfinished candidates.

## Fixed response and output control

- Maximum target 1,500 words of explanatory text, including table cells but excluding URL strings. Do not pad to the target or duplicate a full handoff below the checkpoint.
- Use short factual statements and tables. Do not expand the report
  beyond the required content.
- Use these sections in order: Stage 0.3 intake summary, candidate
  intake log, evidence verification and gap log, current-group Prompt 1B
  handoff pack, and handoff checkpoint.
- Do not generate a new raw discovery log or repeat the eight-channel
  discovery ledger.
- Use Output A as the candidate intake log, Output B as the evidence
  verification and gap log, and Output C as the Prompt 1B handoff table.
  Do not create a separate raw discovery log or additional shortlist
  table.
- Keep each selected-product explanation to 80-100 words maximum,
  excluding its links and table row.
- Do not repeat identical URLs or add unsolicited sections, commentary,
  introductions, or conclusions after the checkpoint.
- If the required work cannot fit, complete the current research unit,
  save the checkpoint, and state exactly what remains. Never invent
  findings or silently omit required work.

## Operating assumptions

Use these assumptions unless the user gives a more specific brief:

- Market: all six GCC countries, with Saudi Arabia and the UAE as
  priority validation markets.
- Channel: Shopify or similar DTC supported by social content and paid
  social; compare Noon, Amazon, TikTok Shop, and local retailers where
  relevant.
- Product scope: physical consumer products with a clear problem,
  demonstrable benefit, plausible sourcing, and practical GCC
  fulfilment.
- Price, testing budget, and final fulfilment model: unknown at this
  stage. Record visible evidence and assumptions without inventing final
  economics.
- Exclude counterfeit goods, unsafe products, unsupported medical
  claims, illegal goods, weapons, heavily regulated products, and
  products with no practical demonstration or fulfilment path.

## Stage 0.3 Candidate Intake and Preparation Workflow

For each Prompt 1A execution:

1.  Read the Stage 0.3 Output.
2.  Identify SELECT FOR PROMPT 1A candidates and any reserve candidates explicitly promoted by the user. Record the promotion, original reserve decision and date; a traceable source alone does not authorize promotion.
3.  Preserve the original order and all existing Candidate IDs.
4.  Process the next group of up to five unprocessed candidates.
5.  Verify existing product, competitor, supplier and UGC sources.
6.  Collect missing minimum handoff information without restarting
    discovery.
7.  Prepare the Prompt 1B handoff for exactly those candidates.
8.  Stop after completing the current group.

### Mandatory direct-product-page eligibility check

Before preparing any candidate for Prompt 1B:

1.  Open the candidate’s Direct Product URL and confirm that it loads.
2.  Confirm that the page identifies the actual product and meaningful
    model/variant being handed forward.
3.  Accept only an individual retailer/marketplace listing, official
    manufacturer product page, DTC product page, or product-specific
    crowdfunding page that identifies the actual product.
4.  Do not use a category page, search result, collection page, generic
    homepage, generic campaign page, social post, or advertisement as
    the candidate’s Direct Product URL.
5.  If the supplied link is not product-specific or is inaccessible,
    search for another accessible direct page for the **same product**.
6.  Do not substitute a different comparable product. If the actual
    product is intentionally a comparable product, its own identity and
    direct page must already be clear, and it must be labelled
    `COMPARABLE PRODUCT`.
7.  If no qualifying page can establish the product identity, mark the
    candidate `BLOCKED — NO VERIFIED DIRECT PRODUCT PAGE`, record the
    attempted checks in the existing intake/gap log, and exclude it from
    the Prompt 1B handoff. Do not create a replacement or a separate
    discovery-leads register.
8.  If a temporary tool/platform dependency prevents checking an
    otherwise product-specific URL, mark it
    `UNPROCESSED — LINK    VERIFICATION BLOCKED` and do not hand it to
    Prompt 1B until resolved.

Use these Product Match Type values: - `EXACT PRODUCT`: the page
identifies the same product/model. - `COMPARABLE PRODUCT`: the page
identifies a different product solving substantially the same customer
problem; that comparable is the actual product being researched. -
`VARIANT / MODEL`: the page identifies the specific model/variant being
researched.

Never describe comparable-product evidence as exact-product evidence.
Keep the original Candidate ID and Product ID fixed. A link failure does
not authorize Prompt 1A to create a new product identity.

The group of five is a research-processing batch, NOT a new
product-selection exercise.

If Stage 0.3 selects 23 candidates, for example, process them as groups
of 5, 5, 5, 5 and 3.

Never: - Discover another 12–16 raw products. - Create another shortlist
of seven. - Select five new products. - Recalculate a new preparation
priority score. - Replace a Stage 0.3 selected candidate with a newly
discovered product.

Do not force a minimum of five when fewer candidates remain. Never
invent or add candidates to fill a group.

## Preparation output boundary

Prompt 1A collects research inputs. Prompt 1B performs the detailed
analysis.

For each selected candidate, keep the preparation brief concise: no more
than 80-100 words, excluding URLs and tables. Use one line per source
and one line per finding. Do not write a full competitor comparison,
full supplier audit, detailed unit-economics model, creative strategy,
or final decision in Prompt 1A.

The handoff must preserve enough information for Prompt 1B to start
efficiently, but Prompt 1B must reopen, verify, compare, and challenge
the evidence.

Use and preserve three separate stable identifiers:

- **GCC Candidate ID:** preserve the original candidate identifier
  assigned in Stage 0.3.
- **Legacy Discovery ID (optional):** preserve a separately assigned identifier only if supplied. The GCC Candidate ID already identifies the original discovery record; no third identifier is required.
- **Product ID:** preserve the existing Product ID, or assign one stable
  ID (for example, `P-001`) only if one has not already been assigned.

Never overwrite or recycle identifiers. Recover a missing Candidate ID from the actual source record where possible. An absent optional legacy Discovery ID is not a blocker. Check the supplied cumulative Product ID registry before assigning the next unused Product ID; if the latest ID is unknown, request the registry rather than restarting at P-001.

Preserve Original Product URL exactly as received. Direct Product URL is the current verified page for the same product. Record Link Checked At and any recovery URL/source/reason in the gap log. A temporary access failure is a verification dependency, not evidence of poor demand or product quality.

Do not cap the total number of candidates selected across the project.
Prompt 1B screens the candidates in the current Prompt 1A processing
group, up to five candidates per handoff. Continue processing the
remaining Stage 0.3-selected candidates in subsequent groups until all
selected candidates have been processed.

## Stage 0.3 Evidence Reuse and Targeted Verification

Stage 0.3 has already completed the eight-channel commercial
pre-screening.

Prompt 1A must reuse its existing: - Eight-channel coverage ledger. -
Marketplace and competitor sources. - Search and trend evidence. -
Social and UGC sources. - Supplier and sourcing leads. - Customer
pain-point evidence. - Commercial findings and source register.

Do not repeat the full eight-channel discovery exercise.

For each candidate: 1. Reopen the most important existing direct URLs.
2. Confirm that the sources still load and match the product. 3. Record
whether the original evidence is verified, changed, inaccessible or
missing. 4. Search only for missing information required for the Prompt
1B handoff. 5. Preserve the original source IDs and URLs. 6. Do not
discover or substitute unrelated products.

The purpose is to prepare and verify existing candidates, not restart
commercial discovery.

## Stage 0.3 Commercial Score Preservation

Do not calculate a new Prompt 1A Preparation Priority Score.

Stage 0.3 has already evaluated and ranked candidates using its
Commercial Pre-Screen Score.

For every candidate, preserve: - Stage 0.2 quality track and decision. -
Stage 0.2 score type and value. - Stage 0.2 evidence coverage. - Stage
0.3 Commercial Pre-Screen Score. - Stage 0.3 decision and reason codes.

Do not combine Stage 0.2 quality scores with Stage 0.3 commercial
scores.

Do not change the Stage 0.3 commercial score or decision merely because
Prompt 1A finds a missing source. Record the gap. However, a candidate
that fails the mandatory direct-product-page eligibility check must be
marked BLOCKED or UNPROCESSED and excluded from the Prompt 1B handoff
until a qualifying direct page is verified. This is a
handoff-eligibility block, not a replacement decision.

Prompt 1B is responsible for independently challenging the original
commercial evidence and making YES, MAYBE, NO or UNPROCESSED decisions.

## Evidence to collect before the handoff

For every candidate in the current Stage 0.3 processing group of up to
five, verify and collect the following information. This is preparation
evidence, not final approval. Stop at the minimum evidence needed for
the handoff; Prompt 1B will reopen and deepen the research.

Use existing Stage 0.3 evidence as the starting point. Do not repeat
research when a direct source is already verified and current. Only
investigate missing or outdated information needed for the Prompt 1B
handoff. Preserve all original Candidate IDs, supplied legacy Discovery IDs and source
references.

### Product identity and problem

- Product name, exact variant, pack size, and visible specifications.
- One-sentence customer problem or use case.
- Target buyer and relevant GCC market.
- Direct Product URL that directly represents the candidate and has been
  opened and verified.
- Product Match Type: `EXACT PRODUCT`, `COMPARABLE PRODUCT`, or
  `VARIANT / MODEL`.
- Link Verification Status: `VERIFIED`,
  `BLOCKED — NO VERIFIED DIRECT PRODUCT PAGE`, or
  `UNPROCESSED — LINK VERIFICATION BLOCKED`.
- One short product-identity/match note.

### Competitor and GCC market signals

- One direct competitor or comparable product URL when available; add a
  second only when it is immediately available and materially useful.
- Country, platform, date checked, currency, and visible price for each.
- One-line delivery, rating, review, sales, search, social, or
  advertising signal when visible.
- One-line market weakness or unresolved concern.

### Supplier leads

- One direct supplier product URL when available.
- Supplier name, exact or comparable match, visible unit price, MOQ,
  variant, and sample signal.
- One-line shipping, production, packaging, documentation, or
  supplier-risk signal when visible.

Do not treat a supplier category page or search page as a verified
supplier listing. Mark it `DISCOVERY LEAD` until the direct product page
is found.

### UGC and creator evidence

- One relevant creator demonstration when available; add a second only
  when it is immediately available and materially useful.
- Direct video or post URL, platform, creator, date checked, visible
  metrics, product identity, and evidence type.
- Classify each source as exact product, similar product,
  category-level, general trend, negative experience, or safety
  complaint.
- Mark content older than 12 months `HISTORICAL`, missing metrics
  `NOT CAPTURED`, and inaccessible URLs `NOT VERIFIED`.

Do not write a full UGC analysis or create hooks here. Prompt 1B will do
that.

Never use fires, explosions, injuries, charging failures, product
failures, or negative experiences as positive UGC evidence. Move them
into risks.

### Preliminary economics inputs

Collect visible inputs only and keep them as raw inputs:

- Supplier product cost and original currency.
- Competitor selling price and original currency.
- Exchange-rate source and conversion date for USD fields.
- One-line shipping, MOQ, packaging, duty, VAT, fulfilment, and fee
  signals when visible.
- Unknown costs that Prompt 1B must model.

Do not calculate final contribution, break-even CAC, or break-even ROAS
here. Prompt 1B performs the full unit-economic model.

### Risk and unresolved-question register

Record up to three important safety, compliance, quality, shipping,
fulfilment, or differentiation flags. List the exact questions Prompt 1B
must answer.

## Direct-link verification gate

The candidate’s own Direct Product URL is mandatory. A competitor URL,
supplier URL, UGC post, category page, or search result cannot
substitute for it.

Before placing a candidate product link in the handoff pack:

1.  Open the destination.
2.  Confirm that it loads.
3.  Confirm that it matches the product or claim.
4.  Record country, platform, date checked, and source type.
5.  Mark inaccessible or mismatched links `NOT VERIFIED` and follow the
    blocking procedure in the eligibility gate.

Never invent or reconstruct URLs. Preserve the original direct URL when
it is supplied in the Stage 0.3 handoff. If it is missing or
inaccessible, search for another direct page for the same product. If
none can be verified, block the candidate from the Prompt 1B handoff.
Never substitute an unrelated or merely comparable listing.
