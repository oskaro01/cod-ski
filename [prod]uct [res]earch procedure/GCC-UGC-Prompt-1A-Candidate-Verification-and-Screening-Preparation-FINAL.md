# Prompt 1A: GCC Product Candidate Verification and Full-Screening Preparation

## Purpose

Use this prompt only after GCC Stage 0.3 Commercial Pre-Screening has selected candidates for Prompt 1A.

The sole product input is the Stage 0.3 Output E — Selected Prompt 1A Candidate Handoff.

Prompt 1A is responsible for preparing already-selected Stage 0.3 candidates for Prompt 1B full screening. It is NOT responsible for discovering another set of products, creating a new shortlist, or selecting replacement candidates.

Process the Stage 0.3 selected candidates in their existing order, in groups of up to five candidates per handoff.

For each candidate:
- Preserve its original GCC Candidate ID.
- Preserve its Stage 0.1 Discovery ID and original source references.
- Preserve its Stage 0.2 track, decision, score type and evidence coverage.
- Preserve its Stage 0.3 commercial score and selection decision.
- Assign one stable Product ID (P-001, P-002, etc.) if not already assigned.
- Verify the existing product, competitor, supplier and UGC links.
- Collect missing minimum handoff information where possible.
- Carry unresolved questions into Prompt 1B.

Do not autonomously discover new products, replace selected candidates, or reject candidates based on a new preparation score.

If a Stage 0.3 candidate has missing evidence, record the gap instead of silently replacing it.

This research is specifically focused on the six GCC markets:

1. United Arab Emirates (UAE)
2. Saudi Arabia (KSA)
3. Kuwait
4. Qatar
5. Bahrain
6. Oman

All product research, market validation, demand assessment, competitor analysis, pricing research, customer feedback analysis, and commercial evaluation must be conducted with these GCC markets as the target market.

- Prioritize GCC-specific marketplaces, retailers, suppliers, customers, search trends, and social-commerce evidence.

- Record the country, platform, currency, source URL, and research date for relevant findings.

- Do not treat non-GCC marketplace listings, ratings, reviews, sales figures, or popularity as proof of GCC demand or GCC customer satisfaction.

- International sources may be used for supporting technical specifications, manufacturer information, product documentation, and global supplier discovery, but clearly distinguish them from GCC market evidence.

- Suppliers may be located outside the GCC, provided their ability to supply the target GCC market is properly evaluated.

- Assess GCC-specific shipping, landed costs, delivery feasibility, returns, warranty, product compliance, and applicable country requirements.

- Never assume that evidence from one GCC country automatically applies to all six countries.

- If GCC-specific evidence is unavailable, explicitly record the gap rather than substituting unrelated international evidence.

## Execution control

The complete Stage 0.3 handoff is mandatory input. If it is missing, do not begin candidate verification or invent a candidate pool. Request the Stage 0.3 handoff before proceeding.

- Execute only targeted verification of existing Stage 0.3-selected candidates and preparation of the current-group Prompt 1B handoff. Do not perform new product discovery or candidate selection.
- Keep the final handoff compact and do not repeat the same source or finding in multiple sections.
- Preserve every completed GCC Candidate ID, Original Discovery ID, and Product ID. Never restart a completed batch or assign a duplicate ID.
- If the response is approaching its limit, stop after the last completed research unit and emit a compact checkpoint listing completed IDs, remaining work, and the next action. Do not silently skip unfinished candidates.

## Fixed response and output control

- Target 1,200-1,500 words, excluding URLs and compact table cells.
- Use short factual statements and tables. Do not expand the report beyond the required content.
- Use these sections in order: Stage 0.3 intake summary, candidate intake log, evidence verification and gap log, current-group Prompt 1B handoff pack, and handoff checkpoint.
- Do not generate a new raw discovery log or repeat the eight-channel discovery ledger.
- Use Output A as the candidate intake log, Output B as the evidence verification and gap log, and Output C as the Prompt 1B handoff table. Do not create a separate raw discovery log or additional shortlist table.
- Keep each selected-product explanation to 80-100 words maximum, excluding its links and table row.
- Do not repeat identical URLs or add unsolicited sections, commentary, introductions, or conclusions after the checkpoint.
- If the required work cannot fit, complete the current research unit, save the checkpoint, and state exactly what remains. Never invent findings or silently omit required work.

## Operating assumptions

Use these assumptions unless the user gives a more specific brief:

- Market: all six GCC countries, with Saudi Arabia and the UAE as priority validation markets.
- Channel: Shopify or similar DTC supported by social content and paid social; compare Noon, Amazon, TikTok Shop, and local retailers where relevant.
- Product scope: physical consumer products with a clear problem, demonstrable benefit, plausible sourcing, and practical GCC fulfilment.
- Price, testing budget, and final fulfilment model: unknown at this stage. Record visible evidence and assumptions without inventing final economics.
- Exclude counterfeit goods, unsafe products, unsupported medical claims, illegal goods, weapons, heavily regulated products, and products with no practical demonstration or fulfilment path.

## Stage 0.3 Candidate Intake and Preparation Workflow

For each Prompt 1A execution:

1. Read the Stage 0.3 Output.
2. Identify candidates marked Stage 0.3 SELECT + RESERVE / RESEARCH candidates with at least one traceable evidence source..
3. Preserve the original order and all existing Candidate IDs.
4. Process the next group of up to five unprocessed candidates.
5. Verify existing product, competitor, supplier and UGC sources.
6. Collect missing minimum handoff information without restarting discovery.
7. Prepare the Prompt 1B handoff for exactly those candidates.
8. Stop after completing the current group.

The group of five is a research-processing batch, NOT a new product-selection exercise.

If Stage 0.3 selects 23 candidates, for example, process them as groups of 5, 5, 5, 5 and 3.

Never:
- Discover another 12–16 raw products.
- Create another shortlist of seven.
- Select five new products.
- Recalculate a new preparation priority score.
- Replace a Stage 0.3 selected candidate with a newly discovered product.

Do not force a minimum of five when fewer candidates remain. Never invent or add candidates to fill a group.

## Preparation output boundary

Prompt 1A collects research inputs. Prompt 1B performs the detailed analysis.

For each selected candidate, keep the preparation brief concise: no more than 80-100 words, excluding URLs and tables. Use one line per source and one line per finding. Do not write a full competitor comparison, full supplier audit, detailed unit-economics model, creative strategy, or final decision in Prompt 1A.

The handoff must preserve enough information for Prompt 1B to start efficiently, but Prompt 1B must reopen, verify, compare, and challenge the evidence.

Use and preserve three separate stable identifiers:

- **GCC Candidate ID:** preserve the original candidate identifier assigned in Stage 0.3.
- **Original Discovery ID:** preserve the original Stage 0.1 discovery identifier (for example, `D-B01-001`).
- **Product ID:** preserve the existing Product ID, or assign one stable ID (for example, `P-001`) only if one has not already been assigned.

Never confuse, overwrite, or reuse one identifier as another. Do not invent a replacement GCC Candidate ID or Original Discovery ID when either is missing; record the gap and request or recover the original identifier.

Do not cap the total number of candidates selected across the project. Prompt 1B screens the candidates in the current Prompt 1A processing group, up to five candidates per handoff. Continue processing the remaining Stage 0.3-selected candidates in subsequent groups until all selected candidates have been processed.

## Evidence to collect before the handoff

For every candidate in the current Stage 0.3 processing group of up to five, verify and collect the following information. This is preparation evidence, not final approval. Stop at the minimum evidence needed for the handoff; Prompt 1B will reopen and deepen the research.

Use existing Stage 0.3 evidence as the starting point. Do not repeat research when a direct source is already verified and current. Only investigate missing or outdated information needed for the Prompt 1B handoff. Preserve all original Candidate IDs, Discovery IDs and source references.

### Product identity and problem

- Product name, exact variant, pack size, and visible specifications.
- One-sentence customer problem or use case.
- Target buyer and relevant GCC market.
- Product or listing URL that directly represents the candidate.
- One short exact-match note.

### Competitor and GCC market signals

- One direct competitor or comparable product URL when available; add a second only when it is immediately available and materially useful.
- Country, platform, date checked, currency, and visible price for each.
- One-line delivery, rating, review, sales, search, social, or advertising signal when visible.
- One-line market weakness or unresolved concern.

### Supplier leads

- One direct supplier product URL when available.
- Supplier name, exact or comparable match, visible unit price, MOQ, variant, and sample signal.
- One-line shipping, production, packaging, documentation, or supplier-risk signal when visible.

Do not treat a supplier category page or search page as a verified supplier listing. Mark it `DISCOVERY LEAD` until the direct product page is found.

### UGC and creator evidence

- One relevant creator demonstration when available; add a second only when it is immediately available and materially useful.
- Direct video or post URL, platform, creator, date checked, visible metrics, product identity, and evidence type.
- Classify each source as exact product, similar product, category-level, general trend, negative experience, or safety complaint.
- Mark content older than 12 months `HISTORICAL`, missing metrics `NOT CAPTURED`, and inaccessible URLs `NOT VERIFIED`.

Do not write a full UGC analysis or create hooks here. Prompt 1B will do that.

Never use fires, explosions, injuries, charging failures, product failures, or negative experiences as positive UGC evidence. Move them into risks.

### Preliminary economics inputs

Collect visible inputs only and keep them as raw inputs:

- Supplier product cost and original currency.
- Competitor selling price and original currency.
- Exchange-rate source and conversion date for USD fields.
- One-line shipping, MOQ, packaging, duty, VAT, fulfilment, and fee signals when visible.
- Unknown costs that Prompt 1B must model.

Do not calculate final contribution, break-even CAC, or break-even ROAS here. Prompt 1B performs the full unit-economic model.

### Risk and unresolved-question register

Record up to three important safety, compliance, quality, shipping, fulfilment, or differentiation flags. List the exact questions Prompt 1B must answer.

## Direct-link verification gate

Before placing a link in the handoff pack:

1. Open the destination.
2. Confirm that it loads.
3. Confirm that it matches the product or claim.
4. Record country, platform, date checked, and source type.
5. Mark inaccessible or mismatched links `NOT VERIFIED`.

Never invent or reconstruct URLs. Preserve the original direct URL when it is supplied in the Stage 0.3 handoff. For every Stage 0.3-selected candidate, preserve the direct product or listing URL already recorded in that handoff; if it is missing or inaccessible, record the gap rather than substituting an unrelated URL.
