# Part 1: The Core Skills

## 1. Product Research: Find a Testable Problem

Product research is not just finding something that looks popular. You are looking for a product with:

- A clear customer problem
- Visible demand or useful demand signals
- A simple demonstration
- Enough margin after real costs
- Low operational risk for a first test
- A believable way to stand out

The first research sheet ranked products such as ice face rollers, pet mats, faucet extenders, and pet hair removers. That was a wide discovery list. Later validation narrowed the business to Swedish dishcloths, silicone stove gap covers, and satin pillowcases.

### Questions to ask

1. Who has the problem?
2. What are they using now?
3. Why would they switch?
4. Can the product be demonstrated in a few seconds?
5. What can go wrong after purchase?
6. Is the demand signal current and credible?
7. Can the product make money after shipping, fees, returns, and advertising?

### Important distinction

**Demand proof** means people appear to buy or search for the category. It does not prove that your exact supplier or offer will succeed. You still need a sample, accurate costs, and a reason to choose your store.

## 2. Competitor Analysis: Learn the Market Before Entering It

The competitor assignment compared three products against three competitors each. The useful structure was:

| Area | What to look for |
|---|---|
| Price | Normal price, sale price, bundles, shipping |
| Reviews | Rating, review count, repeated complaints |
| USP | The reason customers choose that seller |
| Weakness | A real gap you can improve |
| Positioning | The space you want to own |
| Proof needed | Photos, instructions, compatibility, demonstrations |

A competitor's weakness is not automatically your opportunity. It becomes an opportunity only if your product, supplier, offer, or page can actually solve it.

For Swedish dishcloths, the positioning was practical and beginner friendly: an easy reusable kitchen routine with a 10-pack for rotation. The message avoided unsupported claims such as "zero waste," "best," or "guaranteed."

### Useful sentence

> The goal is not to copy the competitor. It is to understand the buyer's expectations, identify avoidable friction, and choose a position we can prove.

## 3. Product Validation: Decide Whether to Test

Validation combines demand, economics, and risk.

The Swedish dishcloth recommendation was:

- Target offer: 10-pack
- Target selling price used in the report: $14.99
- Estimated margin before advertising: 50.3% in the validation model
- Main risks: thin material, odor, poor durability, and category saturation
- Decision: GO

### What "GO" means

It means the product deserves a controlled test. It does not mean guaranteed success, guaranteed demand, or an approved final supplier.

### Validation checklist

- Demand signal checked
- Competitors checked
- Supplier availability checked
- Sample quality plan created
- Costs estimated
- Main returns and complaint risks identified
- Safe marketing claims defined
- First test offer selected

## 4. Suppliers and Landed Cost

Supplier research compares more than unit price. Check:

- MOQ: minimum order quantity
- Unit price and quantity breaks
- Supplier history and verification signals
- On-time delivery and reorder signals
- Product customization
- Sample price and lead time
- Packaging options
- Shipping terms
- Defect and replacement policy
- Certifications only when relevant and verifiable

The supplier shortlist selected Ningbo Heyuan for Swedish dishcloths because it offered a strong trust profile and a low test-friendly MOQ. The cost comparison then used a planning model for 1,000 retail packs.

### Main formulas

```text
Product cost per pack = unit cost per piece x pieces per pack

Customs per pack = product cost per pack x duty rate

Landed cost per pack = product cost + shipping + customs + packaging

Gross profit before fulfillment and ads = selling price - landed cost

Contribution before ads = selling price - product cost - packaging - shipping/fulfillment - payment fees

Break-even ad cost per order = contribution before ads
```

The assignment's landed-cost model estimated **$4.73 per 10-pack** for the preferred supplier. That number did not include every future expense. Always label assumptions clearly and confirm customs classification, shipping quotes, and platform fees before ordering.

### Key lesson

High gross margin is not the same as high net profit. Advertising, payment fees, fulfillment, returns, taxes, discounts, and damaged inventory can reduce the final result.

## 5. Supplier Emails: Ask Before You Buy

The three templates covered:

1. Initial inquiry
2. Price negotiation
3. Sample request

A good supplier message is specific. Ask for:

- Exact material and dimensions
- Product and packaging photos
- Sample cost and shipping
- MOQ
- Price at several quantities
- Production time
- Shipping options and terms
- Custom logo or packaging requirements
- Defect handling
- Real certifications or test reports, if needed

Do not negotiate only on price. A slightly higher cost may be better if quality, packaging, lead time, and communication are stronger.

## 9A. Meta Ads: Build a Full-Funnel Campaign

The Meta campaign structure document used three audience stages for the Swedish dishcloth product:

| Stage | Audience | Main job | Main KPI |
|---|---|---|---|
| TOF | Cold interests | Introduce the problem and product | Cost per landing page view, CTR |
| MOF | Engaged users and video viewers | Explain use and build confidence | Cost per add-to-cart |
| BOF | Product visitors, carts, and checkout users | Bring high-intent shoppers back | Cost per purchase, ROAS |

### Meta account structure

Campaign
  -> Ad set: audience, budget, placements, schedule
      -> Ad: copy, image/video, headline, CTA, landing page

The draft used a 14-day testing period and a planning budget of $50 per day: 50% for cold traffic, 30% for warm engagement, and 20% for retargeting. That is a test structure, not a guarantee that the budget is profitable.

### What changes by funnel stage

- **TOF:** Show familiar kitchen messes and introduce the reusable dishcloth.
- **MOF:** Explain wet, wring, wipe, rinse, dry, and the usefulness of a 10-pack rotation.
- **BOF:** Handle hesitation, remind visitors of the product, and make the purchase path clear.

### Meta metrics to know

- CPM: cost per 1,000 impressions
- CTR: clicks divided by impressions
- Cost per landing page view: spend divided by landing page views
- Cost per add-to-cart: spend divided by add-to-cart events
- CPA: spend divided by purchases
- ROAS: attributed revenue divided by ad spend

Before judging performance, check that the Meta Pixel or equivalent tracking is recording the events correctly. If tracking is broken, the dashboard can make a good or bad campaign look misleading.

## 12. Technical SEO: Can Search Engines Crawl and Understand the Site?

The Ecommerized audit found repeated metadata across important pages and reviewed indexability, canonical tags, robots.txt, sitemap behavior, and structured data opportunities. The technical SEO assignment then classified Search Console issues from the supplied exports.

### The main issue types and plain-English meaning

| Issue | Meaning | Typical action |
|---|---|---|
| Not found (404) | URL does not exist | Fix internal links, redirect a moved page, or leave a genuine removed page as 404/410 |
| Alternate page with proper canonical | Google selected another canonical | Check that the chosen canonical is intentional |
| Blocked by robots.txt | Crawlers are disallowed | Unblock valuable pages; keep crawl waste blocked when intentional |
| Excluded by noindex | Page tells Google not to index it | Remove noindex from valuable pages; keep it on private or low-value pages |
| Page with redirect | URL sends users elsewhere | Link directly to the final URL and keep redirects purposeful |
| Soft 404 | Page looks empty or unavailable but returns 200 | Add useful content, redirect, or return the correct status |
| Other 4xx | Client-side error | Find the exact status and fix the page or link |
| Duplicate without selected canonical | Similar pages compete | Choose and declare the preferred URL |
| Server error (5xx) | Server failed to respond correctly | Fix hosting, application, or API failure |
| Crawled, currently not indexed | Google saw it but did not include it | Improve usefulness, uniqueness, links, and page quality |
| Indexed though blocked by robots.txt | Google indexed a URL it cannot crawl now | Decide whether to unblock or remove it properly |

### Technical SEO workflow

```text
Find affected URL
  -> inspect HTTP status, robots, noindex, canonical, and content
  -> choose the correct action
  -> fix in the store/theme/server/Search Console
  -> test the live URL
  -> request validation and monitor the result
```

Do not "fix" every warning automatically. Some exclusions are intentional. The goal is a clean index that contains useful canonical pages.

## 13. Google Ads and Performance Max

The Google Ads assignment introduced account hierarchy and campaign setup.

### Account hierarchy

```text
Manager account
  -> Google Ads account
      -> Campaign
          -> Asset group
              -> Headlines, descriptions, images, logo, video, audience signals
```

### Performance Max basics

Performance Max is a goal-based Google Ads campaign that can use multiple Google placements. The campaign needs:

- A business and account
- A goal such as sales or website traffic
- Conversion tracking when available
- Budget and bidding method
- Location and language
- Final URL
- Text, image, logo, and video assets
- Audience signals or search themes as learning inputs

### Metrics to know

```text
CTR = clicks / impressions
CPC = ad spend / clicks
Conversion rate = conversions / clicks
CPA = ad spend / conversions
ROAS = revenue attributed to ads / ad spend
```

Do not judge a campaign from one metric alone. A low CPC is not useful if traffic does not convert. A high ROAS may still be weak if tracking is incomplete or margins are too small.

The draft campaign was kept product-specific, with the dishcloth page as the final URL and no unsupported claims. A draft or paused campaign should not be described as a live performance result.

## 14. Google Merchant Center and Product Feeds

Merchant Center connects a product catalog to Google. The assignment had four practical parts:

1. Make the store public and usable.
2. Verify and claim the website.
3. Connect a product source or feed.
4. Review and fix product or account issues.

### Product feed fields to remember

| Field | Why it matters |
|---|---|
| `id` | Stable unique product identifier |
| `title` | Clear product name |
| `description` | Accurate product information |
| `link` | Public product landing page |
| `image_link` | Public product image |
| `availability` | Must match the store |
| `price` | Must match the store and currency |
| `brand` | Real brand name |
| `gtin` or `mpn` | Real identifiers when available |
| `identifier_exists` | Use only when identifiers genuinely do not exist |
| `multipack` | Shows that the product contains multiple units |
| `google_product_category` | Helps Google classify the product |

### Common issues

- Website not verified or claimed
- Missing shipping or return policy
- Price mismatch
- Availability mismatch
- Missing or invented identifiers
- Unclear, blocked, or promotional images
- Product page unavailable
- Misrepresentation or missing trust information

### Core lesson

Merchant Center is a data quality and trust exercise. Your product feed, product page, price, availability, shipping, returns, and business information must agree.
