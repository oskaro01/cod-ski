# Ecommerce Copy Frameworks

## Evidence Before Copy

Prefer this evidence hierarchy:

1. Verified product specifications, policies, test results, and offer terms
2. Customer reviews, support questions, search queries, and survey language
3. Competitor positioning and repeated category conventions
4. Explicit assumptions that still require validation

Use voice-of-customer language to understand motives and objections, but do not copy testimonials, competitor phrasing, or claims without permission and support.

## Customer Awareness

Adapt the message to the buyer's current awareness:

| Stage | Buyer mindset | Content priority |
|---|---|---|
| Unaware | Does not recognize the problem | Make the situation recognizable without forcing a sale |
| Problem-aware | Feels the friction but lacks a solution | Clarify the problem and desired outcome |
| Solution-aware | Knows solution categories | Explain the mechanism and relevant tradeoffs |
| Product-aware | Knows the product or brand | Prove differentiation and answer objections |
| Most aware | Ready but waiting for a reason | Make the offer, terms, urgency, and next step clear |

Do not use purchase-heavy language too early or educational detours too late.

## Framework Selection

Use a framework as a thinking aid, not a visible formula.

| Situation | Useful structure | Core sequence |
|---|---|---|
| Product page | Benefit-proof-objection-CTA | Desired outcome, differentiator, evidence, risk reduction, action |
| Cold ad | PAS | Problem, agitation, solution |
| Warm ad or email | AIDA | Attention, interest, desire, action |
| Feature-heavy product | FAB | Feature, advantage, customer benefit |
| Educational content | Problem-mechanism-options | Define problem, explain why it happens, compare solutions |
| FAQ | Direct answer-context-next step | Answer first, clarify limits, link to action |
| Review response | Acknowledge-specific response-resolution | Recognize experience, address issue, offer next step |

## Format Specifications

### Short Product Description

- Aim for 40-80 words unless the channel dictates otherwise.
- Lead with the product's primary customer outcome.
- Include one or two differentiating facts and a natural CTA.
- Do not compress so aggressively that compatibility or safety becomes misleading.

### Long Product Description

- Use a clear value proposition, benefit-led sections, proof, use cases, specifications, objection handling, and CTA.
- Make headings scannable.
- Distinguish verified facts from persuasive interpretation.
- Include important usage, compatibility, care, and limitation information.

### Blog Introduction

- Match the reader's search intent immediately.
- Establish the problem, stakes, and useful promise of the article.
- Avoid long scene-setting, dictionary openings, and unsupported statistics.
- Preview what the reader will learn without summarizing the entire article.

### Email Subject Lines

- Match the email's actual content and audience awareness.
- Produce meaningful variation across benefit, curiosity, specificity, urgency, and directness.
- Avoid deceptive `Re:` or `Fwd:`, false urgency, fake personalization, and excessive punctuation.
- Respect requested character limits and include preheaders when useful.

### Ad Headlines

- Communicate one idea quickly.
- Build variants around distinct angles, not synonym swaps.
- Pair promise with credible specificity.
- Avoid prohibited or unverifiable claims, personal-attribute targeting, and fake scarcity.

### Social Captions

- Match platform behavior and the requested goal.
- Use a strong opening, useful or entertaining middle, and clear interaction or action.
- Use hashtags and emoji intentionally, not as filler.
- Distinguish organic community content from direct-response promotional content.

### FAQ Answers

- Answer the question in the first sentence.
- Add only relevant context, caveats, and next steps.
- Use calm language for shipping, returns, compatibility, ingredients, or safety concerns.
- Never invent policy details.

### Review Responses

- Reference the customer's specific experience.
- Thank positive reviewers without sounding automated.
- For negative reviews, acknowledge the issue without arguing, admitting unsupported liability, or exposing private data.
- Offer a practical resolution path and move account-specific details to a private channel.

### Brand Voice Guide

Include:

1. Brand role and audience relationship
2. Three to five voice pillars
3. "We sound like / we do not sound like" contrasts
4. Tone adaptations for sales, education, support, and complaints
5. Vocabulary, mechanics, emoji, humor, and urgency rules
6. At least three before-and-after examples

## Benefit and Proof Ladder

Move from facts to value carefully:

1. **Feature:** What the product objectively has or does.
2. **Functional benefit:** What that enables.
3. **Emotional benefit:** How the buyer may feel or what friction it removes.
4. **Proof:** Why the buyer should believe the claim.

Example:

- Feature: Double-wall stainless-steel bottle.
- Functional benefit: Helps maintain drink temperature longer than a single-wall bottle.
- Emotional benefit: Fewer disappointing warm drinks during a busy day.
- Proof: Use only a verified test result or specification supplied by the brand.

## Offer and Objection Logic

Build conversion copy around the complete decision, not the product alone:

- **Value:** What meaningful outcome does the buyer receive?
- **Differentiation:** Why choose this option or mechanism?
- **Proof:** What reduces disbelief?
- **Friction:** What effort, learning, compatibility, or delivery concern exists?
- **Risk:** What could make the purchase feel unsafe or regrettable?
- **Terms:** What price, bundle, guarantee, shipping, or return details affect the decision?
- **Action:** What should the buyer do next, and why now?

Prioritize objections by likelihood and impact. Answer the highest-risk objection where the buyer encounters it instead of hiding all objections in an FAQ.

## Common Failure Patterns

- Generic copy that could describe any product
- Feature dumping without customer value
- Too many messages competing for attention
- Unsupported superlatives or invented proof
- Treating every audience as ready to buy
- Repeating the same headline angle in different words
- Excessive urgency, exclamation marks, emoji, or buzzwords
- Omitting limitations that drive returns or complaints
