# Positioning Map Guide

Use a positioning map to show where each competitor sits and where the user's product can stand out.

## Common Axes

Choose axes that match the evidence:

- Price vs Review Strength: useful when competitors are easy to compare by visible price, rating, and review count.
- Price vs Differentiation: useful when features, bundles, materials, design, or guarantees differ.
- Premium vs Practical: useful for home, beauty, and pet products where buyers compare value, look, and trust.
- Problem-Solving Strength vs Purchase Risk: useful for gadgets where compatibility, leakage, batteries, or product quality can create buyer hesitation.

## ASCII Map Template

Use this format when the user needs a markdown document:

```text
High differentiation
^
|                  [Competitor B]
|        [Our product]
|
| [Competitor A]                 [Competitor C]
+------------------------------------------------> Higher price
 Low price
```

Keep labels short. Explain the map below it in 2-4 sentences.

## Recommendation Pattern

For each product, write:

- Best open position: one sentence.
- Pricing lane: one sentence.
- Product-page proof points: 2-4 bullets.
- Ad angle: one sentence.
- Risk to monitor: one sentence.

Example:

Best open position: Own the affordable, beginner-friendly version with clearer compatibility guidance than higher-priced competitors.

Pricing lane: Stay below premium branded options, but avoid the cheapest tier if reviews show leakage or durability issues.

Product-page proof points:
- Compatibility chart
- Close-up material photos
- Before/after demo
- Clear return or replacement promise

Ad angle: Show the exact problem in the first 2 seconds, then demonstrate the product solving it without exaggerated claims.

Risk to monitor: Complaints about fit, leakage, or weak materials can quickly hurt conversion and reviews.
